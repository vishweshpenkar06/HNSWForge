## Phase 2 baseline — naive HNSW

Measured on this machine (i7-12700H, Java 25, `-Xmx4g`, single query thread, k=10, m=16, efConstruction=200). Synthetic uniform vectors; 200 queries for recall, 200 queries × 10 passes for QPS.

| n | dim | efSearch | build_s | QPS | recall@10 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10,000 | 32 | 100 | — | — | 0.995 |
| 100,000 | 32 | 100 | 52.35 | 3,262 | 0.9265 |
| 100,000 | 32 | 200 | 52.35 | — | 0.9775 |
| 100,000 | 128 | 100 | 167.70 | 1,364 | 0.4330 |

Honest caveat: uniformly random vectors in 128 dimensions are intrinsically harder for ANN than structured SIFT descriptors; the 0.4330 figure is a property of that synthetic data, not evidence of a graph bug. At 32 dimensions the same code reaches 0.9775 at efSearch=200. Real SIFT1M numbers are recorded in Phase 6.

This table is the baseline for the Phase 4 optimization waterfall.

## Phase 3 — parameter sweep

Command: `java -cp hnswforge-eval/target/classes:hnswforge-core/target/classes dev.hnswforge.eval.Main sweep --dataset synthetic --n 10000 --m 8,16,32 --efc 100,200 --efs 20,40,80,160 --k 10 --out results/sweep_synthetic.csv`
Chart: `docs/charts/recall_vs_qps_synthetic.png` (generated by `scripts/plot_results.py`).

Selected points at n=10,000, dim=32, k=10:

| m | efConstruction | efSearch | recall@10 | QPS | build_s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 100 | 20 | 0.846 | 46,793 | 0.94 |
| 8 | 100 | 160 | 0.974 | 9,264 | 0.89 |
| 16 | 100 | 20 | 0.963 | 37,335 | 2.27 |
| 16 | 100 | 80 | 0.993 | 10,475 | 2.17 |
| 32 | 100 | 20 | 0.992 | 22,299 | 10.0 |
| 8 | 200 | 160 | 0.984 | 8,903 | 1.50 |

Analysis: `efSearch` is the strongest query-time knob — at m=8/efC=100, raising it from 20 to 160 lifts recall from 0.846 to 0.974 but costs about 5x QPS. Raising `M` from 8 to 16 at efSearch=20 lifts recall from 0.846 to 0.963 with only a modest QPS drop (46.8k to 37.3k), but build time more than doubles (0.94s to 2.27s); M=32 reaches 0.992 recall but build time jumps to 10s, so returns diminish sharply. Raising `efConstruction` from 100 to 200 gives a small recall gain (e.g. 0.974 to 0.984 at m=8/efSearch=160) while increasing build time by roughly 60%, with little effect on query QPS. Practical default: M=16, efC=200, efS=100-160.
